1. Context and Grading Logic

The repository currently contains only the assignment brief and the two course guides. There is no code. This plan turns the Module 2 Assignment Project V2 brief into an executable engineering programme: ingest a raw dataset, model it as a star schema in a cloud warehouse, transform it with an ELT tool, prove data quality with automated tests, analyse it in Python, orchestrate the whole thing, and present it to a mixed executive audience.

Both course guides converge on the same grading logic, and this plan is built around it:

  • Marks come from the evidence chain, not from the tool count. Source and contract, then raw load, then transformation lineage, then quality gates, then optimised query, then insight, then recommendation, then caveat.
  • Roughly 80% of effort goes to the pipeline, architecture, access control, quality and reproducibility. Roughly 20% goes to a small number of decisive analyses. One decisive chart beats ten disconnected ones.
  • Complexity only earns credit when it solves a stated constraint and leaves operational evidence. Kafka, Spark and Redis are deliberately out of scope; they appear only in the future-expansion section, with the threshold that would justify them.
  • Every table must have a declared grain, a primary key, an owner and a description before any SQL is written.

1.1 The primary business case

BUSINESS CASE 3 — DELIVERY PERFORMANCE. Management question: where and when are deliveries late? The course guide is explicit that a strong project picks one defensible business case early and lets it determine the grain, the dimensions, the tests and the final chart. This is ours. Every design decision below is traceable to it.

The business case decomposes into four questions we must be able to answer with evidence:

#QuestionWhy an executive cares
D1WHERE are deliveries late? Which customer states, which seller states, and which routes between them?Determines which carrier contracts, regional hubs or seller SLAs to renegotiate first.
D2WHEN are deliveries late? Which months, weeks and days of week, and is the delay trend improving or worsening?Determines seasonal capacity planning and whether past logistics investment worked.
D3WHAT does lateness cost us? Its association with review score, and the revenue exposed to late delivery.Converts a logistics metric into money and satisfaction, which is what a CFO or COO will act on.
D4HOW BAD is the estimate itself? Is the promised delivery date systematically wrong, and by how much?A promise nobody can keep destroys trust even when the actual delivery time is acceptable. Fixing the promise is often cheaper than fixing the logistics.

1.2 Supporting analyses required by the brief

Section 5 of the assignment brief separately mandates monthly sales trends, top-selling products and customer segmentation. These are delivered in full, and each is framed through the delivery lens so the report tells one story rather than four:

  • Monthly sales trend, cross-read against the monthly late-delivery rate, so revenue and service quality are seen together.
  • Top products and categories, with their delivery-delay and review profile attached, so we can identify categories that sell well but ship badly.
  • Customer segmentation by RFM, cross-tabulated with delivery experience, so we can quantify how much high-value customer revenue is exposed to late delivery.

3. Target Architecture

Data flows in one direction through the layers below. Each numbered step is something a person can rerun on its own, and each shaded gate is a place where bad data stops rather than continuing downstream.

1 SOURCEKaggle Olist export. Nine CSV files, about 120 MB, 1,550,922 rows. Optionally enriched with Brazilian public holidays from BrasilAPI.
2 INGESTingestion/load_olist.py. Builds a manifest holding the sha256, byte size and true CSV record count of every file.
3 LANDINGGoogle Cloud Storage. gs://<PROJECT_ID>-olist-raw/olist/raw/v1/ - an immutable, replayable copy of exactly what was downloaded.
GATE 1Great Expectations, on the files, before the load. Expected files present, column set and order, row count against the manifest, non-null keys, money ranges, categorical domains. On failure nothing is loaded at all.
4 RAWBigQuery olist_raw.* Explicit schemas, autodetect disabled, source fields preserved as-is, plus _batch_id, _loaded_at, _source_uri and _source_sha256.
5 STAGINGdbt stg_* models. Rename, cast, deduplicate, translate categories, and collapse geolocation to one row per zip prefix.
6 INTERMEDIATEdbt int_* models - the fan-out controls. Payments aggregated to order grain, reviews reduced to one per order, item lines rolled up, and the delivery milestone arithmetic.
7 MARTSdbt dim_*, fct_* and agg_* models. The consumption contract: three facts, four dimensions and five aggregates, partitioned and clustered.
GATE 2dbt build - 161 tests, interleaved with the models. Uniqueness, not-null, referential integrity, accepted values, numeric ranges, milestone chronology and source-to-mart reconciliation. On failure the marts do not publish.
8 SERVINGNotebooks, dashboard, report and deck. Jupyter via SQLAlchemy, a Streamlit dashboard, the written report and the executive deck - all reading the marts layer only.
ACROSS ALLOrchestration and CI. A Dagster asset DAG with a daily schedule runs steps 1 to 7 in dependency order and stops downstream work when a gate fails. GitHub Actions checks every pull request.

4. Data Model: Star Schema

GRAIN FIRST. The single rule that governs this section: write down what one row of each fact table represents before writing any SQL. The grain determines the primary key, which measures are additive, and which joins are safe. Every documented Olist failure comes from skipping this step.

Because the primary business case is delivery performance, fct_orders is the headline fact table: lateness is an order-level property, not an item-level one. fct_order_items remains the revenue backbone, so that money questions and delivery questions can be answered from the same warehouse without mixing grains.

4.1 Grain declarations

ModelOne row representsKeyMeasures and notes
fct_order_itemsOne item line within one orderorder_item_key = order_id || '-' || order_item_iditem_price, freight_value, item_gross_value. Partitioned by order_purchase_date; clustered by customer_key, product_key, seller_key.
fct_paymentsOne payment recordpayment_key = order_id || '-' || payment_sequentialpayment_value, payment_installments, payment_type. Kept as a separate fact so it can never multiply item revenue.
fct_orders (PRIMARY FACT for this business case)One order, modelled as an accumulating snapshot across its delivery milestonesorder_idMilestones: purchased, approved, handed to carrier, delivered, estimated. Delivery measures: approval_days, seller_handover_days, carrier_transit_days, total_delivery_days, promised_delivery_days, delay_vs_promise_days, promise_slack_days, is_late (boolean), lateness_bucket. Money: item_count, order_revenue, order_freight, order_payment_total. Experience: review_score.
dim_customerOne personcustomer_key (from customer_unique_id)customer_state and customer_city are first-class here: they are the WHERE of the business case. Also zip prefix, region grouping, first and latest order date. SCD Type 1.
dim_productOne productproduct_key (product_id)English category name, weight and dimensions (both plausible drivers of transit time), photo count. SCD Type 1 plus a dbt snapshot for Type 2 evidence.
dim_sellerOne sellerseller_key (seller_id)seller_state and seller_city are first-class: origin is half of a delivery route. Also zip prefix and region grouping. SCD Type 1.
dim_dateOne calendar day, 2016-01-01 to 2018-12-31date_keyDay, week, month, quarter, year, weekday flag, Brazilian public-holiday flag if the optional enrichment is built.

4.2 Fan-out controls: the four Olist traps

These are the specific, documented ways this dataset silently produces wrong numbers. Each has a countermeasure and a test.

TrapWhat goes wrongCountermeasure
Payment fan-outJoining raw payments to order items multiplies rows: N items times M payment lines. Revenue inflates, sometimes by 2 to 3 times, and the error looks plausible.Aggregate payments to order grain in int_order_payments before any join. Enforced by a reconciliation test: sum(fct_order_items.item_price) must equal sum(stg_order_items.price).
Review fan-outolist_order_reviews contains repeated review_id values and some orders with more than one review.Deduplicate to one review per order in int_order_reviews (latest review_creation_date, tie-broken on review_id). Enforced by a unique test on order_id.
Customer identitycustomer_id is issued per order, not per person. Using it for RFM splits one repeat buyer into several one-time customers and destroys the segmentation.dim_customer is built on customer_unique_id. customer_id is retained only as a degenerate attribute on the order. Enforced by a unique test on customer_key.
Geolocation cardinalityolist_geolocation has about 1M rows and many rows per zip-code prefix. Joining it directly to customers or sellers explodes the row count.Collapse to one row per zip prefix in stg_geolocation (median latitude and longitude, modal city and state) before it enriches any dimension.

4.3 Physical design and the cost benchmark

  • fct_order_items and fct_orders are partitioned by order_purchase_date (DATE) and clustered by customer_key, product_key, seller_key, the three columns our analyses filter and join on most.
  • Queries filter the partition column directly and never wrap it in a function, so partition pruning can occur. SELECT * is banned in models and notebooks.
  • Deliverable evidence: a three-way benchmark of the same business query against an unpartitioned copy, a partitioned copy, and a partitioned and clustered copy, recording bytes processed, duration and slot-milliseconds, with the result cache disabled. This is the optimisation proof the course guide asks for and the answer to "how do you control BigQuery cost?"

4.4 Slowly changing dimensions, stated honestly

Olist is a static historical export, so no dimension actually changes over the life of the project. dim_customer, dim_product and dim_seller are therefore SCD Type 1, and we say so. One dbt snapshot is built on product category to demonstrate Type 2 mechanics (valid_from, valid_to, current flag) as course evidence. We will describe it as a demonstration of capability, not as a pretence that history is being captured.

4.5 How the business case drives every model decision

This is the table to point at in Q&A when asked "why is your schema shaped like that?"

Business-case needModel decision it forcesConsequence if we got it wrong
Lateness is a property of an order, not of an item linefct_orders is an accumulating snapshot at order grain, holding every milestone and every derived duration. Delivery measures never live on fct_order_items.Averaging delay across item lines would silently weight multi-item orders more heavily and skew every regional figure.
"WHERE" means both destination and origindim_customer and dim_seller both carry state, city and a region grouping. An order route is a customer-state and seller-state pair, exposed in agg_delivery_by_route.Reporting only by customer state would blame the destination for problems originating with a distant seller.
"WHEN" means calendar time and trenddim_date is a full conformed date dimension, and fct_orders is partitioned by order_purchase_date so month-over-month scans stay cheap.Deriving months with string functions on a timestamp would prevent partition pruning and make trend queries scan the whole table.
Late delivery must be linked to satisfactionReviews are deduplicated to one per order in int_order_reviews and joined onto fct_orders at matching grain.Joining raw reviews would duplicate orders and distort both the average review score and the revenue total.
Late delivery must be linked to moneyOrder revenue is aggregated from item grain into fct_orders once, in an intermediate model, and reconciled against staging.Joining items to orders inside the analysis would multiply revenue by the item count.
A promise can be wrong even when delivery is finedelay_vs_promise_days and total_delivery_days are stored as separate measures, never collapsed into one "late" flag.A single boolean would hide the far cheaper fix: correcting the estimate rather than the logistics.

6. Ownership and Access Control

Five or more people sharing one GCP project is the most likely source of accidental damage in this assignment. The controls below are not bureaucracy; they are what stops two people from redefining the same table on the same afternoon.

6.1 Role assignment

Analytics / Communications OwnerNotebooks, metric dictionary, report, executive deck, insight cardsProject: roles/bigquery.jobUser. Dataset Viewer on olist_marts, read-only.

6.2 Non-negotiable rules

  • One person creates the raw dataset and performs the upload. Everyone else reads it. This prevents conflicting schema changes on the same table.
  • Every member develops dbt into their own dbt_<name> dataset using a dev target. Only a passing dbt build promotes anything to olist_marts.
  • Local development authenticates with user OAuth (gcloud auth application-default login) so every BigQuery job is attributable to a person. No service-account key is ever downloaded to a laptop.
  • Exactly one service account exists, for GitHub Actions, with the minimum roles it needs. Its key lives only in GitHub Secrets.
  • Git is the version-control system for SQL, YAML, Python and documentation. BigQuery tables have no version history: if it is not in Git, it can be lost.
  • Never commit .env, any service-account JSON key, anything under data/, dbt target output, or notebook outputs containing credentials.

Step F — Run the first data load

Platform Owner, once the Phase 3 code exists. Approximately 10 minutes for the first run.

make ingest

This single command computes a manifest (file name, byte size, sha256, row count) for all nine CSVs; uploads them to the GCS landing prefix; runs the Great Expectations file gate; loads each file into olist_raw with an explicit schema and no autodetect; attaches _batch_id, _loaded_at and _source_uri metadata columns; and prints a reconciliation table comparing source row counts with BigQuery row counts.

The load uses WRITE_TRUNCATE per batch, so running it twice is safe and produces identical row counts. Demonstrating that is itself a graded piece of evidence: idempotency.

Verify:

make ingest        # run it a second time
bq query --use_legacy_sql=false \
  "SELECT COUNT(*) FROM `<PROJECT_ID>.olist_raw.orders`"

The count must still be 99,441 after the second run. Commit the reconciliation log to docs/evidence/.

9.1 The fault-injection demonstration (required)

Both course guides identify this as the strongest single piece of evidence available. It is a required Phase 6 deliverable, not an optional extra.

  1. Run dbt build with the pipeline healthy and save the full passing output.
  2. On a branch, inject two controlled faults into a copy of the staging data: a duplicated order_item_key, and a negative price.
  3. Run dbt build again. Capture the failing tests, the failure counts, and the actual offending rows that dbt stores.
  4. Show that the downstream mart models did not build, so the gate held.
  5. Repair the data or the rule, rerun, and capture the green result.
  6. In the report, state plainly which business misjudgement this gate prevented: duplicated keys inflate revenue, and negative prices corrupt every average.

11. Orchestration and CI

11.1 Dagster

Dagster models the pipeline as assets, meaning the data produced, rather than as tasks, so lineage and materialisation status come for free. The asset graph:

asset 1olist_source_files. Manifest plus the upload to the GCS landing prefix.
asset 2raw_quality_gate. The Great Expectations checkpoint. Raising here stops every asset below it.
asset 3olist_raw_tables. BigQuery load jobs and the row-count reconciliation.
assets 4+dbt assets, via dagster-dbt. stg_* to int_* to dim_/fct_/agg_, with every dbt test surfacing as an asset check.
outputanalytics_exports. The materialised KPI tables the notebooks and dashboard read.
  • A daily schedule triggers the job. Retries are configured on the ingest assets only, because they are idempotent; transformation failures should surface, not silently retry.
  • A failing quality gate stops downstream materialisation. We will capture a screenshot of exactly this, because a cron job that runs everything regardless of failure is not orchestration, and the course guide is explicit on this point.
  • Each run records run_id, start and end time, input and output row counts, status and errors.

11.3 Interactive dashboard (Streamlit)

The notebooks are the analyst’s surface; they are not something a COO will open. The dashboard is the same analysis for someone who will not run Jupyter, and it is what gets demonstrated live during the presentation.

TabAnswersNotable content
WhereWhich destinations and which routes are late?Late rate by destination state against the national average; an origin-to-destination region heat map; the routes carrying the most late orders, with the revenue behind each.
WhenWhich months, and is it improving?Order volume against late rate by purchase month; a first-half versus second-half comparison stated as a percentage-point change; the stage decomposition showing whether days are lost in payment approval, seller handover or carrier transit.
What it costsWhat does lateness cost in satisfaction and revenue?Review score by lateness bucket; the same penalty recomputed within each state as a control; revenue exposed to late delivery by region; and whether the promise itself is padded or optimistic.
Business KPIsThe three metrics the brief mandatesMonthly sales with the late rate overlaid; a revenue-versus-late-rate scatter that surfaces categories which sell well but ship badly; RFM segments with their delivery experience attached.
Data qualityCan these numbers be trusted?Runs the revenue reconciliation, the order-grain check, the person-grain RFM check and an app-versus-model agreement check LIVE against the warehouse, then states plainly what each of the two pipeline gates blocks.

Two design rules, both inherited from the architecture rather than from Streamlit convention:

  • It reads the marts layer only. No CSV, no raw tables, no local files. Every number comes from a dbt model that has tests attached, so the dashboard, the notebooks and the report cannot disagree with one another.
  • It does not redefine metrics. Definitions live in the metric dictionary and are implemented in dbt. Interactive filtering does force the late rate to be recomputed against fct_orders, because a pre-aggregated model cannot be sliced by an arbitrary filter combination; that SQL is confined to a single constant in the app, and the Data quality tab asserts live that it still agrees with agg_delivery_monthly. The dashboard therefore cannot silently drift from the pipeline.
  • The shared connection module lives at the repository root and is imported by both the notebooks and the dashboard, so the two cannot end up reading different datasets while both appear correct.
  • Run with make dashboard. It opens on localhost:8501 and needs the same user OAuth credentials as everything else; no service-account key is involved.

13.1 Q&A preparation: every member must be able to answer

Likely questionOur answer
Why delivery performance as the business case?It is the one question in this dataset that connects operations, customer satisfaction and revenue in a single chain, and every element of it is measurable from the source data rather than inferred. It also gives the schema a reason to exist: order grain, route geography and a conformed date dimension are all forced by it, not decorative.
How do you define "late"?delay_vs_promise_days greater than zero, measured only on orders that actually reached the customer. We report the promise error and the actual transit time as separate measures, because a wrong promise and a slow delivery need different fixes.
Why Olist rather than the other datasets?It has the richest set of related tables, so it demonstrates multi-table modelling, revenue reconciliation and customer segmentation. Instacart has no money columns; London Bicycles has no customer entity, which would make the required segmentation analysis impossible.
Why is the fact grain one order item?Product and seller analysis requires item-level detail, and order_id alone is not unique at that level. Order-level metrics live in a separate fact so the two grains never mix.
How do you know the revenue figure is right?Reconciliation tests assert that total revenue in the mart equals total revenue in staging. Payments are aggregated to order grain before any join, so fan-out cannot inflate the number.
Why not Spark or Kafka?The data is 120 MB and static. A cluster or a broker would add operational cost with no benefit. We have documented the thresholds, namely data beyond single-node memory or a decision that loses value within minutes, that would justify each.
What does BigQuery cost you?Effectively nothing: the free tier covers 1 TB scanned per month and this dataset is 120 MB. We also have a benchmark showing bytes scanned before and after partitioning and clustering.
What happens when a test fails?Error-severity rules block publication and preserve the failing rows. Warnings publish with an explanation. We can show you a failure and the fix.
Are these findings causal?No. They are descriptive and correlational. Where we suggest a causal mechanism we label it as a hypothesis and propose an A/B or quasi-experimental test.
Who can change the data?One person owns raw uploads. Everyone else develops in an isolated dataset and promotes only through a passing build. Local access is user-attributed OAuth; the only service account is for CI and has the minimum permissions it needs.
